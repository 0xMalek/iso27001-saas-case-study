I have been building my understanding of ISO/IEC 27001 by applying it to a practical portfolio case study.

For my first project, I used a fictional 25-person cloud software company preparing to answer enterprise customers' security questions. The focus was implementation planning: understanding the business, defining the scope, assessing risks, and explaining which actions should come first.

The case study includes an inventory of 10 assets, 12 assessed risks, a Statement of Applicability extract covering 21 selected controls, and a 90-day implementation roadmap. Each risk has an owner, a reasoned rating, and a treatment or acceptance decision.

I also worked through a simulated access review covering 27 assignments across three systems. The records contained a departed employee with active access, an unjustified administrator role, and a role mismatch. The follow-up verifies three corrections in revised synthetic records while preserving a legitimate, independently approved administrator account.

The main lesson: a corrected account is only one part of the work. The process that allowed the issue also needs attention, and its effectiveness needs to be checked later. Proposed controls and estimated future risk scores should not be presented as achieved outcomes.

This is an educational simulation, not a certification engagement or a claim of a fully implemented ISMS. My next learning step is to build on this foundation with an internal audit simulation.
